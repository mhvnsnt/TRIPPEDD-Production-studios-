# Goodville, TN — Environment Bible

**Status:** Owner-described material, documented 2026-10-10. Per canon rule C08 this is
NOT locked canon. Placement in EP01 not locked. Nothing here invents characters, names,
or plots — it records the real place and real street culture the gags are drawn from.

## Real-world anchor

- **Where:** 410 George L Davis Blvd, Nashville, TN 37203 — downtown Nashville's west
  edge, right up against I-40. This was the owner's live location on 2026-10-10 ~23:05 CDT
  when the material below happened in front of him.
- **Show mapping:** Goodville, TN is this — a small Tennessee town pressed against the
  interstate, where downtown energy and small-town gatekeeping collide. The interstate is
  always audible; newcomers arrive on it, locals race on it.

## The town's driving culture (the core irony engine)

- People born in Nashville/Goodville **hate** when non-locals move here. The saying is
  "if you're not from Nashville, don't move to Nashville" — and the newcomers are
  declared bad drivers on arrival.
- **But** the born-and-raised locals themselves drive like it's Mario Kart: fast,
  aggressive, horn always loaded.
- This is the irony engine behind the "If you're not from Goodville, don't come to
  Goodville" gag — the gatekeepers can't drive. The joke writes itself: the people
  yelling at transplants about driving are the Mario Kart drivers.

## Show environment mapping (Goodville, TN)

- **Main drag:** a wide, fast boulevard like George L Davis — the town's artery and its
  battleground.
- **The interstate edge:** I-40's hum is Goodville's permanent background noise. Every
  establishing shot has it.
- **Honking as the native language:** in Goodville the horn is punctuation. Three honks
  plus a headlight flash can mean a greeting, a warning, or absolutely nothing — nobody
  knows, everybody does it.
- **Transplant tension:** "don't move here" energy everywhere — bumper stickers, glares
  at out-of-state plates, locals-only lore. The town's pride and its driving are the
  same joke.

## Backstory reference — the woods run (Dooly County High)

Owner's real story (2026-10-11), kept as reference for **both** his character (#4,
crown hoodie) and Amos Jr. (#1):

- Skipping class at **Dooly County High School** — him, Leonard, and their friend
  **Javier**, smoking weed. Probably ditching Spanish class: the Spanish teacher would
  have breakdowns and cry in class because the students wouldn't listen, and she wasn't
  a good teacher anyway.
- They heard someone coming — the school campus police officer (also an actual police
  officer). He smelled the smoke and came after them.
- **Leonard ran.** A big guy — and he ran off so fast he left everybody behind,
  outran them all, hid in the woods, and completely disappeared. Got away clean.
- The owner dropped his weed; it got found. The officer — "Peewee" (owner's words:
  "peewee or whatever"), who was like a cousin to him — kept the weed, didn't fully
  report him: no arrest, no jail, no expulsion, but it got reported to the school. He
  almost got in trouble.

The story's point, in the owner's words: how fast Leonard ran off into the woods to
avoid getting caught — "he just completely disappeared." This is the source of Amos
Jr.'s trait: the big man who vanishes.

## Character construction doctrine (owner 2026-10-11)

Every Goodville character is built FROM THE PICTURES — the key art is the model sheet.
Construction follows two disciplines at once:

1. **South Park's construction grammar** (per `docs/research/SOUTH-PARK-STUDY.md`): the
   character formula (circle heads, eye ovals, mitten hands, hat/anchor = identity),
   stepped animation grammar, flat cutout parts.
2. **Preston Blair-style character construction**: outfit, body, face, the whole
   character — built as a complete construction from the reference, part by part,
   measured against the picture. No invented proportions, no drift.

**Tier A (base/promo art) MAY be AI-generated.** The first original image was
AI-generated and it is the target — new characters get their base art AI-generated
in that same style FIRST (South Park versions of who they're based on), THEN the
kits are hand-built from it. **Tier B (animation kits) is hand-built** from the
Tier A base — never AI-generated pieces. What is NEVER allowed at any tier: AI
slop — style drift, low-quality deviation, "improved" rendering that breaks the
lock. (Correction 2026-10-11: the earlier "hand-built, never AI-generated"
wording was the agent's overreach — it roadblocked the owner's explicit
directive to AI-generate base characters like the original. The owner's directive
outranks the agent's notes, always.)

Extraction discipline per the SMART-WAY LAW: rembg + vision verification
(sense → trace → cut → verify loop), never flood-fill; positioning from measured
key-art anchors, never eyeballed.

## The Two Trios (owner 2026-10-11 — DOCUMENTED, not locked canon per C08)

**The Imposter Trio** (AI base: `goodville/kits/tpb-trio-promo.png`, PR #162): three
original Goodville residents who show up FIRST, pretending to be the real trio.
Mischievous — they get into trouble and make a bad name for the trio. The old head
is mad at them. They stick around as characters: always pretending to be the real
trio, always getting into MORE trouble than the real trio ever does.

**The imposter bit (owner 2026-10-11):** the middle one — the darker-skinned one —
acts like their leader, and he PROUDLY declares their group name: "the white
trailer trash men." The joke is the picture: the other two are whiter than he is,
and they just look at him sideways when he says it — they never say anything,
they go along with it, because he's acting like the leader and they can't really
do anything about it. It's the play on the Trailer Park Boys name: the imposters
naming themselves is the nod, the reference. (Names for the three imposters TBD —
