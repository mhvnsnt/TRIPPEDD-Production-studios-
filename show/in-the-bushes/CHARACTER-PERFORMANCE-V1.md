# In the Bushes — Character Performance v1

## Purpose

The teens are not interchangeable animation placeholders. They are ordinary human kids whose individual personalities should be readable through simple silhouettes, posture, timing, movement and reactions.

This document defines the first performance pass without permanently locking names or final backstories.

## Provisional performance identities

### Teen 01 — Impulsive
- Moves first and thinks second.
- More confident body language.
- Commits quickly once an idea is suggested.
- Runs aggressively and throws with conviction.
- During the Busch transformation, their first instinct is escape.

### Teen 02 — Cautious
- Notices danger earliest or reacts most visibly to it.
- More guarded posture and frequent glances toward the police.
- Hesitates before committing to the beer-throw decision.
- Bigger nervous reactions create contrast with the others.

### Teen 03 — Deadpan
- Lowest visible reaction to ordinary chaos.
- More relaxed posture and smaller movements.
- Observes the other two before acting.
- Comedy comes from pauses, looks and understated reactions rather than constant motion.

These are performance placeholders, not final character names or immutable personalities.

## Animation rules

1. Each teen gets independent timing offsets.
2. A group action should contain individual reactions before and after the shared beat.
3. Running uses alternating arm/leg positions, body lean and changing stride.
4. Panic is expressed through pose, head direction and timing rather than camera shake alone.
5. Hiding requires an actual crouch and peek, not a translated standing silhouette.
6. The beer decision should reveal personality differences before the group points at the bush.
7. The throw should have anticipation, release and follow-through.
8. The Busch reveal should produce three different human reactions, even if those reactions are brief.
9. Reuse motion systems, but do not make every character perform the same motion at the same frame.

## Visual restraint

The characters should remain simple enough to animate cheaply. Personality should come from strong silhouettes, a small number of repeatable clothing/accessory cues, poses, timing and acting—not from excessive detail.

## Canon relationship to the world

The teens remain grounded human characters. They are not inherently magical or cartoonishly bizarre. Busch becoming sentient after the beer is thrown into the bush is the first supernatural breach in the opening. Future unusual elements should have a story or causal reason to exist.
