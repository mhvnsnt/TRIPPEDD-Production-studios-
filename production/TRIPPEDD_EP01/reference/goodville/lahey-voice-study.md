# Lahey Voice Study — for the Goodville old head

**Status:** research only (owner 2026-10-11). No audio generated, no model built.
**Subject:** Jim Lahey (Trailer Park Boys), played by John Dunsworth (1946–2017).
**Purpose:** voice-direction guide for the old head — works whether Tyneshia performs him or we build a style-alike voice.
**Policy:** style-alike PARODY voice only. No cloning of a real performer without consent — Dunsworth died 2017, so cloning is off the table entirely. Everything here describes the *style* to aim at, never the man's actual voiceprint.

> Owner note 2026-10-11: the old head keeps his South Park Canadian flap mouth — "Lahey is Canadian, Trailer Park Boys is Canadian, it's perfect." Tyneshia may voice him. Name TBD by owner + Tyneshia — he is "the old head" until then.

## 1. Jim Lahey — the character

Trailer park supervisor of Sunnyvale, ex-cop (dismissed from the police force — the Boys' fault, which curdled into his life's grudge), raging alcoholic, on-and-off antagonist across 12 seasons (2001–2018). Started as a swaggering confident gatekeeper; alcoholism hollowed him into something stranger and funnier. Bisexual; on-off relationship with his assistant Randy (calls him "Bobandy," especially drunk); daughter Treena, estranged over the drinking.

What makes him more than a drunk joke: Dunsworth played him with total commitment — he found the man's dignity inside the humiliation. The character believes he is the only sane authority in a park of criminals, and Dunsworth lets you see that belief even mid-collapse.

### Dialogue patterns

**Shit-isms ("Shit Talk").** Lahey warns, threatens, and philosophizes through elaborate shit-metaphors, delivered dead seriously. The fan-documented set includes: shitstorm, shitapple ("a shitapple never falls far from the shit tree"), shiticane, shitnami, shit-barometer ("measures the shit-pressure in the air"), shit-winds, shithawks, shitrope, shitline, shitleopard, shitabyss, shitmoths, shitpuppets, shitquake, shitblizzard, "shit clock's tickin'." The engine: take any normal idiom (apple/tree, barometer, winds) and shit-ify it, then deliver it like weather forecasting.

**The liquor speeches.** His grand philosophy: "I AM the liquor" / "It's not me... it's the liquor." When drunk he explains the liquor as a spiritual ideal — giving up one's personality to become one with something higher ("that's why they call liquor 'spirits'"). These speeches are grandiose, rambling, and weirdly coherent — the drunkest man in the room explaining the universe.

**Misquoted erudition.** Like Ricky, he mangles common phrases while trying to sound intellectual — and nobody corrects him. The comedy: supreme confidence + wrong words.

**Sober scheming.** Sober Lahey is the other animal: quiet, conspiratorial, manipulative — plotting with Randy, running cons, playing authority. The shit-talk gets sharper and more deliberate; it's a weapon, not a weather report.

## 2. The three voice ranges

### Range A — DRUNK Lahey (the default old-head mode)

**Vocal qualities:** Heavy slur on consonants (s/t/d go soft), pitch sits low-to-mid and *slides* — sentences start declarative and melt downward. Pace is uneven: rushes through setups, then wallows in the punchline words. Volume swells mid-speech; he'll get suddenly loud on a shit-ism, then drop to a mumble. Texture: wet, gravelly, phlegmy — the voice of a man drinking from the bottle between sentences. Emotional register: grandiose, wounded, philosophical by turns.

**Signature moves:** the long build-up ("Do you feel that... the way the shit clings to the air?"), the pause before the shit-ism lands, the sudden shouted warning ("The shit winds are coming!"). Falls over his own metaphors and keeps going.

**Use for the old head:** tall-tale scenes (the dinosaur story), warnings to the town, any "I've seen some things..." monologue. This is the range the promo already leans on.

### Range B — SOBER/scheming Lahey

**Vocal qualities:** Noticeably sharper. The slur mostly clears; pace steadies; pitch drops a half-step and stays there. Conspiratorial low volume — talks like someone who doesn't want the mic to catch it. The shit-isms get *aimed*: slower, deliberate, menacing. Less philosophy, more threat assessment.

**Signature moves:** quiet asides to a partner-in-crime, the cold open threat ("Shit clock's tickin'"), procedural cop-voice leaking through (he was a cop once — it surfaces when he's giving orders).

**Use for the old head:** scheming scenes, confrontations, any moment he's trying to run something instead of just surviving it.

### Range C — John Dunsworth OUT of character (the coherent old head)

**Vocal qualities:** Warm, articulate, measured. Atlantic Canadian accent, theater-trained diction — clean consonants, full sentences, natural pauses. Pace is calm and unhurried; pitch mid-range, steady, gentle rises for emphasis rather than volume. The total inverse of Lahey: a thoughtful man who chose his words. Fans who met him consistently describe him as chill, sweet, and smart — "a very chill, thoughtful and sweet man."

**Why it matters:** Dunsworth was a serious stage actor — University of Guelph theatre, starring roles at Halifax's Neptune Theatre, years of CBC radio drama (literal voice-acting training), co-founded Halifax's Pier One Theatre in 1971, founded Filmworks Casting in 1987. His natural instrument was a trained, warm, precise voice. His drunk-Lahey was *acting* — he was once kicked out of a McDonald's for "being drunk" while in character for fans and couldn't convince the manager he was pretending.

**Use for the old head:** scenes where he's suddenly, startlingly coherent — calm, reasonable, almost gentle. The comedy writes itself: the town drunk drops the slur for thirty seconds and sounds like a college professor, then slides back. Owner 2026-10-11: "some scenes where he seems real coherent... acting so normal and calm" against his usual self.

### Range-switching direction

The old head's voice is a *continuum*, not three costumes. Drunk (A) is home base; sober (B) is the gear-shift when he's working an angle; coherent (C) is the rare drop that makes a scene land. Direction note for Tyneshia or the voice build: practice sliding A→B→C on a single line — e.g. start a shit-ism drunk, sharpen it sober mid-sentence, land the last three words clean and calm. That's the character's whole arc in one breath.

## 3. Dunsworth's other roles (material to pull in)

- **Dave Teagues, *Haven* (2010–2015)** — mysterious small-town reporter in a Stephen King supernatural drama. Calmer, quieter register than Lahey — the conspiratorial-knowing voice, useful for the old head's "I've seen some things" mode without the slur.
- **Officer McNabb, *Shattered City: The Halifax Explosion* (2003, CBC)** — period cop; the authority-voice Lahey's ex-cop past would surface.
- **Stage work** — Shylock and Charles Manson played *sympathetically* (his words: "I'm always looking for the humanity"). That's the acting note for the old head: never play him as just a joke; he believes every word.
- **CBC radio dramas** — years of voice-only acting; the man knew how to carry a scene with voice alone.
- **The role's origin** — Lahey grew out of Dunsworth's award-winning turn in Mike Clattenburg's short *One Last Shot* (1998, Atlantic Film Festival best performance). The character was built, not improvised.

## 5. Voice-build plan (style-alike parody — NOT a clone)

**Standing policy:** no cloning of a real performer without consent. Dunsworth died in 2017; cloning his voice is permanently off the table. The build target is a *style-alike parody voice*: an original voice that carries Lahey's *manner* (the slur patterns, the shit-ism cadence, the grandiose-collapse arc) without reproducing the man's voiceprint.

**Pipeline (all exists in the workspace):**

1. **Voice-direction brief → human first.** This document IS the brief. Tyneshia is considering performing him — if she does, she records test reads in all three ranges against the promo's temp lines ("I've seen some things...", the dinosaur bit). Human performance is the preferred final; the generated voice is the scratch/stand-in path.
2. **Scratch generation (no clone).** Two allowed routes:
   - *ElevenLabs Voice Design* (text-described voice — "older male, gravelly, slurred, grandiose drunk with sudden volume swells") via the connected `custom.elevenlabs` credential. Description-based, not sample-based — no real voice is copied.
   - *Chatterbox* style tests with a generic older-male reference (NOT Dunsworth audio), steered by the direction notes.
   - *local-voice* (espeak-ng) for timing/placement scratch only — it can't do character.
3. **Range coverage.** Build/test all three ranges as separate direction presets (drunk-default, sober-scheming, coherent-calm), since the old head slides between them mid-scene.
4. **Iterate against reference.** Compare scratch output to the *manner* checklist (§2), not to Dunsworth's recordings — the test is "does it do the Lahey things," never "does it sound like him."
5. **Lock at final mix.** Human performance (Tyneshia or a cast voice) replaces scratch wherever possible; generated style-alike only where a human isn't available. Owner signs off on the final voice.

**What we are NOT doing:** ripping Dunsworth's audio from interviews/episodes as training data, cloning his voice, or marketing anything as "John Dunsworth's voice." The parody exemption covers the *style*; it doesn't grant the man's voice.
