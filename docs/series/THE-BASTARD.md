# THE BASTARD

**Studio:** TRIPPEDD Production Studios  
**Series status:** Developing / future standalone series  
**First intended use:** Embedded segments inside the TRIPPEDD TV show  
**Narrative connection:** Bannon storyline  
**Dialogue profile:** Sparse  
**Primary storytelling:** Physical performance, environment, sound, composition, action, visual motifs, consequence

## Series premise

The Bastard follows a solitary Bannon through a mysterious primal world.

We do not initially know why we are following him.

We do not receive a conventional backstory dump.

We watch him exist.

He walks. He searches. He survives. He observes. He fights. He gets hurt. He encounters things. He makes decisions. He keeps moving.

Pieces of the larger Bannon narrative can surface, but they are allowed to appear as unexplained fragments. The series can eventually reveal that these fragments belong to the same canon while preserving the possibility that this is an alternate timeline, dimension, or version of Bannon.

## Core influences

The creative starting points include:

- *Samurai Jack* — visual storytelling, disciplined silence, episodic wandering, action as character language
- *Primal* — primal physicality, environmental storytelling, sparse dialogue, consequence
- Dark fantasy / graphic-novel visual language
- *Dark Souls* — environmental mystery, implied history, hostile worlds, fragments that invite inference
- The specific comic-art references previously collected by the production team — to be attached to the asset/reference registry when identified

These are **mechanism and mood references**, not instructions to copy another property's surface style.

## The Bastard rule

> **If Bannon can communicate it without talking, he should.**

Dialogue is permitted, but speech should feel consequential because it is scarce.

A Bastard scene should be understandable through some combination of:

- body language
- gaze
- movement
- environment
- sound
- object interaction
- violence/action
- spatial relationships
- repetition
- visual callbacks
- consequences

## The camera follows the mystery

The audience is not promised an explanation for every strange thing.

Instead, the production deliberately creates evidence:

`OBSERVE → NOTICE → QUESTION → FOLLOW → DISCOVER → MISINTERPRET → RECONTEXTUALIZE`

The viewer participates by constructing the missing story.

This connects directly to the TRIPPEDD surreal/psychological grammar: the image can be bait, the contradiction can be the hook, and the association can become the maze.

## Visual grammar

Default visual rhythm:

`ENVIRONMENT → FIGURE → DETAIL → MOVEMENT → INTERRUPTION → RESPONSE → CONSEQUENCE → STILLNESS`

Action should not automatically mean fast cutting. A long walk, a pause, or a character looking at something can carry as much narrative weight as a fight.

### Silence

Silence is an active production element.

Use:

- footsteps
- breathing
- insects
- wind
- distant animals
- clothing movement
- weapon/object handling
- environmental impacts
- room tone
- sudden absence of sound

Music should not constantly tell the audience what to feel.

### Violence

Violence is physical storytelling, not spectacle by default.

A fight should reveal something:

- capability
- desperation
- restraint
- fear
- injury
- adaptation
- moral choice
- exhaustion
- relationship to the environment

The Bastard is connected to Bannon without requiring a one-to-one continuity explanation.

Possible relationships include:

```text

    past?    parallel?  future?
```

Do not resolve the ambiguity simply because the database can represent it. Uncertainty can be an intentional story state.

## How The Bastard enters TRIPPEDD

Before it becomes a standalone show, The Bastard appears as a **series interstitial** inside TRIPPEDD episodes.

A TRIPPEDD episode can abruptly cut from its normal format into a Bastard sequence:

`TRIPPEDD SCENE → HARD CUT → THE BASTARD → HARD CUT → TRIPPEDD SCENE`

The transition does not need to be explained.

The segment may be:

- 10 seconds
- 30 seconds
- 2 minutes
- an entire short narrative

The audience gradually learns that these strange fragments are connected.

## Graduation path

```text
REFERENCE ASSET
      ↓

      ↓

      ↓
RECURRING BASTARD INTERSTITIAL
      ↓
```

When the series graduates, it keeps its own title, episode numbering, continuity metadata, and production identity while remaining a TRIPPEDD Production Studios property.

## Asset pipeline

The Bastard can be built from whatever visual material the studio has available:

- real footage
- 3D animation
- Blender renders
- 2D animation
- generative video
- generated stills
- composited photography
- procedural environments
- hand-authored assets
- open-source tools
- sound recordings
- found/reference material where legally usable

The source of an asset does not determine the identity of the series. The editorial decision does.

Every generated or transformed asset should retain provenance, including its generation method, source/reference assets, software/tool, version, prompt or procedural recipe when available, and editorial purpose.

## Relationship to the TRIPPEDD creative constitution

The Bastard follows the studio's broader principles:

- physical source truth and editorial/subjective truth remain distinguishable
- format changes are allowed when they serve the story
- accidents can become discoveries
- callbacks are production objects, not just jokes
- strange imagery should have a job
- the audience should be trusted to infer
- generated material is a production capability, not automatically the point
- **the decisions are the aesthetic**

## First-stage production target

Do not attempt to build a complete Bastard series before the visual language has been discovered.

First make **small Bastard fragments** from the existing 2D, 3D, generative, screenshot, and reference material. Assemble them experimentally. Identify recurring visual motifs. Test silence. Test physical action. Test the relationship between Bannon and the environment.

The first successful fragment becomes evidence for the next one.

The series should grow out of production rather than being completely specified in advance.

## Studio joke / meta-layer

The Bastard can be treated internally as the studio's “bastard child”: a series created inside TRIPPEDD Production Studios that is initially abandoned into the woods and expected to figure out what it is.

Keep the joke internal to production unless it becomes useful on-screen.
