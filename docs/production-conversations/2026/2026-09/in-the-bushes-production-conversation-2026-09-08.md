## Title discussion

The working title is **In the Bushes**.

The title originated through an improvisational chain of references and wordplay:

- Adult Swim was discussed as a broad reference point for irreverent, economical, strange animation.
- **12 oz. Mouse** was mentioned as a reference for the spirit of low-budget adult animation and a drunk/beer-adjacent premise, explicitly as inspiration rather than something to copy.
- **King of the Hill** was mentioned as another classic Adult Swim reference.
- Hank Hill's association with beer led to **Busch** beer.
- Busch → bushes → the phrase **In the Bushes**.
- The title also naturally suggests something strange happening in a place where nobody is supposed to be looking.

The current preference is to keep the **show title spelled normally as “In the Bushes”**, while using **Busch** as the spelling/reference for the transformed main bush character's name. This distinction is still a creative decision under development, but it is the current direction.

## Core origin premise

A group of teenagers are somewhere they should not be, such as an alley, park, or similar outdoor location. They notice police, parents, or another authority arriving nearby. They have a six-pack of beer, with some beers already opened.

They panic and decide they need to get rid of the beer and run.

They take hurried little sips while trying to move, then throw the six-pack and the opened beers into a nearby bush.

The beer spills through the bush, soaking its branches/leaves and surrounding area.

The beer has a surreal transformative effect: the bush becomes **sentient and conscious** and comes to life. The transformation has a visual, exaggerated comic-book/superhero-like energy — described in conversation as having a “Spider-Man effect” on the bush — while remaining an original transformation concept for this show.

Crucially, the newly conscious bush is also **drunk** because beer is part of the accidental origin of its consciousness.

## Main character naming concept

The transformed bush is the main character.

The user's current naming idea is that the bush's personal name should be spelled **Busch**, matching the beer reference that caused his transformation.

This creates a layered distinction:

- **Show:** *In the Bushes*
- **Main character:** *Busch*
- **Origin:** Busch beer is thrown into the bush and accidentally transforms it.

The beer reference is therefore embedded in the character's identity without requiring the entire show's title to use brand spelling.

## Creative identity

The show should not become “12 oz. Mouse with beer swapped in.” The referenced works are inspiration/reference points for production philosophy, tone, economy, and the tradition of strange adult animation — not source material to reproduce.

The show needs its own:

- mythology
- characters
- visual identity
- comedy engine
- setting logic
- recurring jokes
- dialogue
- stories
- props/assets
- world rules

No copying of existing characters, catchphrases, scenes, assets, distinctive compositions, or story premises.

## Beer as world DNA

Beer can be a recurring element of the world, but the central joke should not simply be “the characters are drunk.”

The stronger idea is that beer is part of the **mythology and accidental history** of Busch's existence.

Possible recurring function:

- Beer is the accidental catalyst for Busch's consciousness.
- Busch may not fully understand why beer affects him or why it is connected to his existence.
- Beer can recur as an object, cultural artifact, source of situations, or strange world detail.
- Other liquids/substances could potentially produce different effects on bushes or plants, but this is an exploration, not canon yet.

An earlier brainstorming possibility was:

- Beer → drunk bush
- Energy drink → hyper bush
- Coffee → paranoid bush
- Water → normal bush

This is currently **unresolved / exploratory**, not established canon.

## Character concept: Busch

Busch is not simply a talking plant. He is a newly conscious being trying to understand existence from the perspective of a bush.

Potential comedy comes from the mismatch between:

- what Busch perceives
- what is actually happening
- how humans interpret him
- his physical limitations as a bush
- his accidental drunkenness
- his sincere attempts to make sense of ordinary events

Possible perception jokes discussed:

- A streetlight could look like the moon.
- A squirrel could be interpreted as a government informant.
- A trash can could be mistaken for an old friend.
- A passing car could seem as though the road itself is moving.
- Busch could recognize someone who has no idea how or why he knows them.

These are examples of the comedy language, not locked scenes.

## Production philosophy

The show should fit the studio's reusable low-cost animation system:

**Write once → build reusable assets → animate with presets → render → publish.**

The bush character should therefore be designed as a reusable production asset with modular:

- body/foliage shapes
- facial features
- eyes
- mouth states
- branch/arm-like gestures where appropriate
- reaction states
- drunk states
- transformation states
- environmental variants

The design should support short segments, interstitials, social clips, and eventually full episodes.
