## 2. Archive architecture reaffirmed

The production system has four distinct layers:

1. **STORYRUNNER CONVERSATION** — the room: user/showrunner + AI/agents discussing, discovering, arguing, planning, revising, and recording creative intent.
2. **CANON / PRODUCTION** — accepted decisions encoded into bibles, schemas, timelines, scripts, manifests, assets, and implementation.
3. **AUTONOMOUS** — independent machine decisions/discoveries made without silently inheriting showrunner-only editorial choices.
4. **EDITORIAL CUTS** — actual assembled outputs, including SHOWRUNNER and AUTONOMOUS variants.

The archive must preserve provenance between these layers rather than flattening them.

## 6. Surreal / psychological grammar preserved

The studio's creative grammar is influenced by the production-room analysis of collage, juxtaposition, Off the Air-style psychological association, Xavier-style absurdist/philosophical instability, and related experimental television language.

Key principle:

> Do not merely show the audience something weird. Give their brain a problem to solve.

Primary grammar:

`NORMALITY → PATTERN → WRONG DETAIL → ESCALATION → ASSOCIATION → REALITY SLIP → RETURN → DENIAL → BUTTON`

Other forms include:

`MUNDANE → PHILOSOPHICAL QUESTION → STUPID ANSWER → CONSEQUENCE`

`OBJECT → CALLBACK → CALLBACK → SYMBOL → DESTRUCTION OF SYMBOL → ORDINARY REPLACEMENT`

`REAL EVENT → SUBJECTIVE WORLD → EXTREME VISUALIZATION → CHARACTER: "that wasn't shit"`

`ARGUMENT → NON SEQUITUR → NEW WORLD RULE → COMMITMENT TO RULE → COLLAPSE`

Every rupture should have a narrative job: psychological association, character perception, thematic counterpoint, escalation, callback, satire, tonal collision, worldbuilding, misdirection, release, or terminal button.

## 7. The Bastard — latest locked direction

**The Bastard does not appear as a normal segment in EP01.** Its first EP01 presence is locked as a final mysterious tag/cliffhanger only.

The proposed EP01 final tag:

- hard tonal transition from TRIPPEDD into a cinematic dark storm;
- Bannon stands alone at the edge of a jagged painted/rocky cliff in heavy rain;
- cinematic low-angle view approximately 25–30 degrees below eye line, rather than an exaggerated 45-degree heroic poster angle;
- Bannon is primarily silhouette, with rain reading through backlight and only limited detail/glint visible;
- one brief lightning event can reveal him momentarily;
- he should feel as though he is watching something specific rather than posing for the camera;
- hold approximately 5–6 seconds;
- no conventional music/sting; rain and low wind carry the scene;
- cut to black;
- brief black/silence beat;
- red lettering fades in: **TO BE CØNTINUED**;
- no explanatory reveal follows.

This is a cliffhanger introduction, not a Bastard episode. The audience should leave with a question about who/what they just saw.

For later episodes, the current planning target is approximately one or two Bastard interruptions per episode. The Bastard can eventually grow into a standalone episodic property while retaining its separate identity from TRIPPEDD.

## 8. The Bastard storytelling language

The Bastard is quiet, stoic, mysterious, and physically narrated. The series follows Bannon alone through jungle/woods/rainforest, caves, ruins, abandoned spaces, and primal environments. Dialogue is sparse. Footsteps, breathing, training, wrestling, grappling, combat, locomotion, injury, fatigue, objects, survival, and silence carry story.

The audience should initially be unsure why it is following Bannon or exactly where this story sits in chronology. The material can operate as alternate/parallel/ambiguous continuity while eventually connecting to the broader Bannon canon.

The progression begins with wrestling/fighting/grappling/training and bodily consequence rather than immediately making killing the central behavior. Violence can become more consequential as the narrative develops.

The audience builds mythology from evidence rather than exposition.
