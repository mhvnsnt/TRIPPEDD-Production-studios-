## Source and visual references

The creator confirmed the reference video is from Osamu Sato's *Eastern Mind: The Lost Souls of Tong-Nou*, Japanese title **東脳 (Tonnō)**. The reference language is early-3D, surreal, psychedelic, spiritual/ritualistic, low-resolution and deliberately strange. Important visual observations include the floating/disembodied-head presentation, partial neck, embossed forehead/back-of-head symbolism, ridged cave-like spaces, and saturated blues, oranges, purples, greens and pinks.

Other cited creative references include *LSD: Dream Emulator* and *Xavier: Renegade Angel*, plus the broader TRIPPEDD language of psychedelic/stony comedy, anthology randomness, Adult Swim-style identity systems, MTV/Comedy Central energy, Robot Chicken/Off the Air-style unpredictability, and dream logic.

## Creative thesis

Life can feel understandable while remaining fundamentally unpredictable; things are not always what they appear to be. God Molecule should express that through dream logic, minimal explanations, surreal transformations, strange geometry, spiritual/mystical visual language, psychedelic color, and deadpan or unexpected comedy.

The production philosophy is **AI co-authored/co-produced, not completely AI-generated**. The creator owns the source material, taste, performance, face, voice, music and final editorial choices. AI and open-source tools are collaborators for asset generation, alternatives, motion, transcription, cleanup, scripting and production acceleration.

## Mars character

Mars is the planned main character. The current concept is based on the creator's own face:

- saturated comic-book/cosmic blue skin with deeper violet/indigo shading;
- the creator's real hair/dreads and facial identity retained;
- solid white eyes;
- a custom embossed forehead sigil inspired by the symbolic language of Tonnō, while remaining the project's own mark;
- floating-head presentation with **partial neck**, rather than an aggressively cut-off face;
- black or near-black space/background with optional small multicolor sparkling stars;
- early-3D / PS1 / Tonnō-inspired rendering language for the eventual 3D version.

The intended presentation is a recurring disembodied/floating head that can exist in space, strange environments, caves, voids, dream rooms, or other impossible settings.

## Critical creator likeness / image-editing rule

The creator explicitly rejected the last two generated Mars images because they drifted too far from the creator's actual likeness and did not use the requested source angles. This is now a **hard production rule** for Mars imagery:

- Use the creator's supplied photographs as the source image whenever an image is being made from the creator's likeness.
- **Do not generate a new-looking person or replace the creator's identity.**
- Preserve the actual face, facial structure, hair/dreads, beard and supplied camera angle/pose unless the creator explicitly asks for a change.
- Apply the requested design, recolor, eyes, sigil, background, lighting, degradation and other effects **on top of the supplied photograph**.
- Do not invent turnaround angles that were not supplied by the creator.
- When a new angle is needed, use a corresponding source photograph from the creator rather than hallucinating a different person or pretending an unsupplied angle is faithful.
- Image generation should function primarily as **editing/effects**, not identity regeneration.
- The creator's statement that the earlier image worked because it used the actual supplied image is the reference standard.

The creator also explicitly requested no new image generation during the documentation turn. Future creator-supplied images can be used for additional Mars angles and effects work.

## Sigil direction

A custom Mars/God Molecule sigil was proposed, incorporating visual ideas associated with Mars, an eye, a spiral/trip motif, and an indole-ring reference. It should remain an original production mark rather than being represented as a verified reproduction of a pre-existing sacred symbol.

The key visual treatment from Tonnō to preserve is **embossing/relief**: the mark should look grown/carved into the character's material rather than pasted on as a flat graphic.

## Rendering language

The target is not modern smooth AI-video polish. The desired aesthetic is intentionally constrained:

- low-poly geometry;
- flat or hard-stepped shading;
- limited palette;
- visible color banding/ridges;
- low frame rate;
- texture warping;
- controlled dithering;
- surreal transitions;
- simple or impossible spaces;
- saturated complementary colors;
- deliberate early-CD-ROM / PS1-era roughness.

The production approach is to generate or construct material freely and then pass it through a consistent visual degradation/normalization pipeline so Blender, AI-assisted imagery, live footage and generated elements can feel as if they came from one fictional machine.

## Technical production plan

Planned toolchain discussed in the conversation:

- Blender for the eventual 3D Mars head, environments, low-poly geometry, lighting and deterministic rendering.
- Godot as a possible interactive/dream-space engine and PS1-style rendering environment.
- FFmpeg for deterministic frame-rate, scaling, palette, dithering and final encoding.
- Open-source image/3D/AI tools as collaborators where useful, with model choices treated as replaceable implementation details.
- Motion capture / facial animation for performance rather than purely generative character acting.
- OpenTimelineIO / the TRIPPEDD Production studios editorial pipeline for durable timeline interchange.

For the photo-stage aesthetic, the intended look can be approximated with a shared palette/banding/dither pass. The true PS1 shader belongs to the 3D stage.

## Pipeline hardening terminology correction

The creator clarified that the prior wording **“guarding commit”** was incorrect. The intended term is **“hardening the pipeline.”**

What that hardening means operationally is not a superficial guard around the code. It means making the production system refuse to spend expensive compute when prerequisites are not proven, preserve successful work, isolate attempts, expose real progress, detect genuine stalls, recover in bounded ways, validate final media/timeline artifacts, and fail closed instead of claiming success from stale telemetry or partial output.

The current production architecture includes source transport preflight, explicit source completeness, authenticated Drive recovery paths, checkpoint/reuse behavior, attempt-isolated telemetry, stale-run handling, final MP4/JSON/OTIO validation, artifact QC, and bounded self-healing. The CI build is green, but CI success alone is not considered production proof.
