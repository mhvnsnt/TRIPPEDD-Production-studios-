# TRIPPEDD Production Conversation Archive

## Purpose

This archive is the recorded **storyrunner / production-room conversation layer** for TRIPPEDD Production Studios.

The production conversation is treated as a first-class creative source. It records the room where the production team discusses what the work means, what to make, why to make it, what was discovered, what was rejected, and how ideas evolve.

This is intentionally distinct from:

1. **Storyrunner conversation** — human + AI production discussion and creative reasoning.
2. **Canon / production documents** — accepted decisions encoded into bibles, schemas, timelines, manifests, scripts, and assets.
3. **Autonomous production record** — decisions and discoveries made independently by agents/workflows.
4. **Editorial cuts** — what ultimately appears in an assembled program.

The archive must preserve relationships between these layers rather than flattening them.

## Permanent recording rule

For work performed through ChatGPT or another connected production agent, every materially relevant production conversation should be captured here or in dated archive entries under `docs/production-conversations/`.

Record:

- user/showrunner statements
- AI/agent responses that materially affect the project
- creative ideas
- story discoveries
- decisions and reversals
- unresolved questions
- production constraints
- references and influences
- technical discoveries that affect production
- disagreements between showrunner and autonomous systems
- provenance of ideas that become canon
- emotional or conceptual reactions when they materially explain why a creative decision matters

Do not fabricate missing transcript text. When historical conversation text is unavailable, preserve a **reconstructed context record** clearly labeled as reconstructed rather than pretending it is a verbatim transcript.

## Storyrunner vs autonomous production

The conversation layer is not supposed to dictate the autonomous cut.

The studio deliberately maintains two perspectives:

### SHOWRUNNER

The human/showrunner conversation establishes intent, asks questions, proposes changes, rejects ideas, accepts discoveries, and provides final creative authority.

### AUTONOMOUS

The autonomous system gets the source material and production rules and is allowed to discover/assemble its own interpretation without silently inheriting showrunner-only decisions that were not encoded as canon or constraints.

The finished outputs can later be compared as two production-team interpretations of the same source.

## The Bastard — current storyrunner record

**The Bastard is not the title of The Walk.** The Walk is the pilot. The Bastard is a separate series property.

The Bastard can initially appear inside later TRIPPEDD episodes as a strange, quiet recurring segment. Over time, those appearances can evolve into standalone shorts and eventually a standalone episodic series produced by TRIPPEDD Production Studios.

The series follows a mysterious, largely solitary Bannon in an alternate/parallel/ambiguous continuity. The audience is not initially told why they are following him or exactly where in Bannon's larger chronology the story belongs.

The intended storytelling language is strongly physical and visual rather than dialogue-driven. Influences discussed include **Samurai Jack**, **Primal**, dark/atmospheric comic-book art, and action/fantasy game language including **Dark Souls**.

The Bastard should emphasize:

- silence and environmental sound
- sparse, consequential dialogue
- solitary movement
- wilderness, jungle, rainforest, caves, ruins, abandoned spaces, and other primal environments
- wrestling, grappling, combat, physical training, locomotion, survival, and bodily consequence
- character revealed through what Bannon does rather than through exposition
- mystery instead of immediate backstory
- progression from training/survival toward more consequential violence as the narrative develops
- occasional references or artifacts from the broader Bannon storyline without requiring the audience to understand that storyline
- alternate-timeline / alternate-dimension / displaced-continuity ambiguity
- abrupt but purposeful changes in production language when the story requires them

The central creative idea is that the audience watches Bannon become/understand himself through physical behavior. The series does not need to explain itself before it has earned the explanation.

### The Bastard visual development influences

The current discussion identifies **David Finch** and **Tony S. Daniel** as particularly important visual reference points, with additional influence from **Jason Fabok, Greg Capullo, Jim Lee, Graham Nolan, Kelley Jones, and Nick Dragotta**.

The desired reference space includes:

- high-contrast, heavily shadowed rendering
- gritty and textured anatomy
- powerful muscular forms
- dramatic lighting
- kinetic action composition
- dense line/detail language where appropriate
- physically convincing anatomy and impact
- dark atmospheric environments
- comic-book visual storytelling translated into animation/3D/generative production rather than copied as a single artist's style

The production should use these references as a **visual design vocabulary and influence map**, not as instructions to impersonate a living artist's exact style.

The user's supplied visual-development material also includes existing screenshots, footage, 2D animation, 3D animation, and generative-AI imagery that can become source material or aesthetic evidence for developing The Bastard.

## Bannon source-model principle

Bannon is based on the user's fictional wrestling-character persona and broader Bannon narrative work. The user has developed Bannon-related material across multiple repositories and ontological systems.

The storyrunner archive should preserve references to those source systems when they materially inform fictional character construction, but private biographical information should not be exposed in production outputs merely because it exists in source context.

The intended character-development inputs can include the already-established Bannon source material, character ontology, wrestling/combat identity, symbolic systems, and other project-specific frameworks when available and appropriate.

## Production-room metaphor

The archive represents the production crew sitting in a room talking about the work.

The conversation is therefore not disposable chat. It is production history.

A later agent should be able to ask:

- Why did we make this choice?
- Who proposed it?
- Was it accepted as canon?
- Was it only an experiment?
- Did an autonomous agent discover it independently?
- What conversation caused the change?
- What source evidence supports it?
- What did we deliberately leave unresolved?

and trace the answer back through the archive.

## Important limitation

This file is the archive's **index and governing policy**, not a claim that every historical ChatGPT message is automatically present in GitHub. Historical material should only be recorded verbatim when the actual transcript is available. Otherwise use clearly labeled reconstructed records based on available project context.
