# Structural Analysis: Professional Show Bible Format
## Source: Johnny Bravo Season 2 Bible (Van Partible & Seth MacFarlane, 1997)
## 47 pages | Internet Archive (public since 2020)

> This analysis describes the ORGANIZATION and FORMAT of a professional
> animation show bible. It does not reproduce copyrighted content.
> Use this as a blueprint for structuring your own original show bibles.

---

## 1. DOCUMENT ARCHITECTURE (5 major sections)

A professional show bible follows this skeleton:

```
1. TITLE / CREDITS PAGE
2. SERIES OVERVIEW (narrative)
3. CHARACTER APPLICATIONS (one per character, standardized format)
4. ENVIRONMENTAL PARAMETERS (setting/world)
5. STORY PREMISES (episode springboards)
```

### Section 1: Title / Credits
- Show name + season/volume
- "BIBLE" designation (tells the reader this is the canonical reference)
- Creator credits (who wrote it, who it's by)
- Sets the tone immediately (this one is comedic in voice)

**Lesson:** The bible's voice matches the show's voice. A comedy bible reads funny.
A drama bible reads serious. The document IS the show on paper.

### Section 2: Series Overview (narrative prose)
- Opens with a vivid character portrait ("Picture, if you will...")
- Answers: Who is [protagonist]? Why does this show exist?
- Describes how the character/show has EVOLVED ("has long since matured from...")
- States the CORE COMEDIC/Dramatic ENGINE (what never changes)
- Introduces what's NEW this season (new characters, new dynamics)
- Ends with the emotional thesis ("And that's what he really wants...")

**Lesson:** This section sells the show to anyone who picks up the bible.
It must work for: new writers, new animators, network executives, new directors.
One page of prose that makes you understand the entire show.

**Structural pattern:**
1. Vivid hook (sensory description)
2. Core question (Who is X? Why X?)
3. Evolution statement (how we got here)
4. Immutable traits (what NEVER changes — the "laws")
5. What's new (this season's additions)
6. Emotional thesis (what the audience should feel)

### Section 3: Character Applications (standardized per-character sheets)
Each character gets an identical-format "APPLICATION FOR SERIES PARTICIPATION"
— framed as a fun in-character questionnaire. Fields observed:

| Field | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| Name | Character identity |
| Occupation | What they do (drives story function) |
| Age | Demographic anchor |
| Height | Physical reference for model sheets |
| Weight | Physical reference |
| Hobbies | Personality texture, story fuel |
| Favorite Color | Visual/design shorthand |
| My Best Quality | Self-perception (often ironic = comedy) |
| Turn On's | Desires (story motivation) |
| Turn Off's | Boundaries (conflict source) |
| Favorite Movie | Cultural reference point |
| My Future | Aspirations (arc potential) |

**Key structural insights:**
- EVERY character gets the SAME form (standardization = comparability)
- The form is IN-CHARACTER (reveals voice through the answers)
- Mix of practical (height/weight for animators) and creative (turn-ons for writers)
- New characters get the same treatment as returning ones
- ~8 characters documented (protagonist, family, supporting, new additions)

**Lesson:** Standardize your character sheets. When every character answers
the same questions, you can instantly compare dynamics, spot redundancies,
and find untapped relationships.

### Section 4: Environmental Parameters (setting/world)
- Dedicated section for the SETTING as a "character"
- City/location name and identity
- Key locations within the world
- Rules of the world (what's possible, what's not)

**Lesson:** The setting gets its own bible entry, not just background detail.
If your show has a distinctive location, document it like a character.

### Section 5: Story Premises (episode springboards)
- List of potential episode concepts (8 in this bible)
- Each is a TITLE + brief premise (not full outlines)
- Titles are evocative and comedic
- Designed as springboards for writers, not finished stories

**Lesson:** A bible doesn't need full episode scripts. It needs PROOF
that the concept generates stories. 8-12 strong premises show the engine works.

---

## 2. FORMATTING CONVENTIONS OBSERVED

- **Section headers** in large display type, often playful
- **Character names** as major headers for each application
- **Two-column or form layout** for the questionnaire fields
- **Hand-drawn/illustrated elements** mixed with type (it's a visual document)
- **Tone consistency:** every page sounds like the show

---

## 3. HOW TO APPLY THIS TO YOUR SHOWS

1. **Copy the 5-section skeleton** (it's industry standard for a reason)
2. **Write your Series Overview** using the 6-step pattern above
3. **Create your Character Application form** (adapt fields to your show)
4. **Document your setting** as its own entry
5. **Write 8-12 story premises** to prove your concept generates episodes
6. **Match the bible's voice to your show's voice**

---

## 4. WHAT THIS BIBLE DOES NOT CONTAIN

Notably absent (these live in separate production documents):
- Model sheets / turnarounds (separate art documents)
- Rig specifications (technical animation docs)
- Full episode scripts (writers' room output)
- Storyboards (visual development)
- Production schedules / budgets (line producer docs)

**Lesson:** A bible is a CREATIVE document, not a production manual.
It answers WHAT and WHY, not HOW and WHEN.
