# EP01 — Autonomous vs Showrunner Experiment

## Purpose

TRIPPEDD is deliberately producing two independent versions of **The Walk** from the same physical source material and the same production toolchain.

This is not redundant automation. It is a creative experiment.

### Lane A — Autonomous

`EP01-AUTONOMOUS`

The AI production system makes editorial decisions without showrunner intervention. It may discover comedy, choose selects, preserve chronology, introduce generated material, and structure the rough cut according to the machine-readable studio grammar.

The point is to observe **how the AI thinks when nobody is steering the edit**.

### Lane B — Showrunner

`EP01-SHOWRUNNER`

The same production system operates with explicit showrunner direction. User/producer decisions, story intent, known callbacks, desired generated sequences, pacing decisions, and editorial corrections are allowed to influence the cut.

The point is to observe **what human-directed intent adds to the machine's production process**.

## Experimental controls

Both lanes should use, wherever practical:

- the same source footage;
- the same physical-source chronology;
- the same media-analysis tools;
- the same transcription stack;
- the same comedy-signal vocabulary;
- the same generated-material provenance rules;
- the same technical render/QC standards.

The principal independent variable is **editorial direction**.

## Compare after both cuts exist

Measure and review:

- total runtime;
- number and duration of source selects;
- source coverage;
- comedy signals selected;
- callbacks preserved or discovered;
- contradictions/reality slips preserved;
- dead air and silence;
- escalation shape;
- format shifts;
- generated-material placement;
- continuity errors;
- accidental comedy lost by either lane;
- moments that become funnier through restraint;
- moments where either lane explains too much;
- ending/button effectiveness;
- subjective viewer reaction.

## Most important comparison questions

1. What did autonomous editing find that the showrunner missed?
2. What did showrunner direction rescue from an otherwise machine-plausible cut?
3. Where did the autonomous cut make a surprising but defensible creative choice?
4. Where did it make a technically coherent but artistically wrong choice?
5. Does human direction improve the episode, or merely make it more familiar to the person directing it?
6. Can the strongest autonomous discoveries be fed back into the studio grammar without destroying their weirdness?
7. Can the strongest showrunner decisions be encoded as reusable production rules rather than remaining one-off instructions?

## Rule

**Do not declare a winner before watching both.**

The autonomous cut is evidence about the production system's creative behavior. The showrunner cut is evidence about collaborative human + AI authorship. The eventual TRIPPEDD workflow should learn from both.
