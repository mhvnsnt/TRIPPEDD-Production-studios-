# TRIPPEDD Show Rule: Reality / Subjectivity

## Canonical rule

**The audience may see the character's subjective reality even when the character does not acknowledge it.**

When a character becomes altered, production language is allowed to mutate with the experience:

`LIVE ACTION → VISUAL DISTORTION → ANIMATION → 3D → BLENDER / CG → IMPOSSIBLE WORLD → ??? → LIVE ACTION`

The transition is storytelling. It is not an excuse to demonstrate technology.

## The joke

The visual experience can be enormous, expensive-looking, impossible, frightening, beautiful, or completely insane while the character's interpretation remains mundane:

> “That shit wasn't even shit.”

The mismatch between **what the audience sees** and **what the character thinks happened** is itself a recurring TRIPPEDD device.

## Production rules

1. **Source truth stays source truth.** Real footage remains identifiable as real footage.
2. **Subjective material is synthetic by declaration.** Animation, AI, Blender/CG, VFX, or reconstructed imagery must carry provenance as generated material.
3. **Do not over-explain the transition.** The audience should experience the shift rather than receive a technical explanation.
4. **Return to reality hard.** A spectacular subjective sequence can be followed immediately by mundane live action.
5. **Character awareness is optional.** The character may deny, ignore, misunderstand, or underreact to what the audience saw.
6. **Spectacle needs narrative purpose.** If removing the generated sequence does not remove meaning, escalation, or a joke, the sequence probably does not belong.
7. **Human editorial approval remains final.** Autonomous agents can discover, plan, generate, and assemble; the showrunner controls canon and release.

## EP01 application

In **The Walk**, the shroomified attempt becomes the first major production-language rupture. The audience gets the full subjective ride through distortion, animation, 3D/Blender/CG, and impossible imagery. The character then returns to live action and treats the experience as worthless.

That makes the final acid beat stronger: the character remembers he has roughly $30 worth of acid, about two and a half double hits of yellow gel tabs, reaches for the next trip, cannot find it, and becomes genuinely sad.

**Do not solve the disappearance. The disappearance is the button.**
