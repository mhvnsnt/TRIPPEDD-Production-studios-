# VFX_TRICK_BIT — Segment Craft Guide

Segment type: `VFX_TRICK_BIT` (see `docs/TRIPPEDD-FORMAT.md`).
Reference: @ctrlcollin (Ctrl+Collin) tiny-self series — studied 2026-10-09.

## The idea

An obvious, simple composite trick played straight. The visible cheapness of the
trick IS the joke. Random, needs no context: a tiny person on a table acting
annoying while the normal-size person reacts, a tiny person living inside a
snack bag, a giant hand menacing a toy. One gag per unit, 5–22 seconds.

## The core technique: tiny-self composite (phone only, free)

No green screen. Two locked-off takes + CapCut's AI "Remove background."

### What to film (one locked phone, must not move between takes)

1. Lock the phone on a tripod or stack of books. Frame a medium-wide shot of a
   table with background visible. Bright, even room light; avoid backlight.
2. **Take 1 — the "giant" plate (~15s):** lean into frame CLOSE to the lens on
   one side. Look down at the table spot where tiny-you will stand, react, talk,
   laugh. End with the swat/grab gesture toward that spot. Keep the table empty.
3. **Take 2 — the "tiny" layer (~15s):** move back so your FULL body is in frame
   against the plainest background available (blank wall/door). Fitted clothes,
   arms slightly away from torso (clean silhouette = clean auto-cutout). Act
   tiny: look up at the camera, gesture big, mime being annoying, react scared
   when the "hand" comes. Match the room lighting.

### Edit in CapCut (free)

1. New project → Take 1 as the base layer.
2. Overlay → Add overlay → Take 2 (picture-in-picture on top).
3. Select overlay → "Remove background" (Cutout). No green screen needed. If
   edges flicker, refine or re-shoot Take 2 against a plainer background.
4. Pinch overlay to ~10–15%, drag onto the table surface where Take 1 looked.
   Line the feet up with the table edge.
5. Trim both layers to the same length. Scrub: tiny-you stands on the table
   while giant-you reacts.
6. **Timing the interaction:** find the swat frame. Split the overlay if needed;
   add a small position keyframe so tiny-you "dodges," or time the swat to land
   on tiny-you's spot then keyframe him shrinking/flying off. A 2–3 frame
   jump-cut sells the impact.
7. Realism pass: drop overlay brightness ~5% to match table shading; add a soft
   dark ellipse under the feet so he sits IN the scene.
8. Export 1080p (CapCut free export is clean, no watermark).

## Variant: forced-perspective practical (no composite at all)

Lock the phone inches from the table. Place the small subject mid-distance, hold
the "giant" hand CLOSE to the lens. One take. Works for: giant hand menacing a
toy, smashing it, holding tiny objects. Add practical fake blood / props as
needed. Trim in CapCut or the native editor.

## Variant: object scaling

Same overlay composite, but the scaled element is an OBJECT, not a person:
giant snack bag with a tiny person inside, giant sponge "disinfecting" a tiny
person, giant hand holding a tiny vehicle/dinosaur. Film the object large in the
plate (close to lens), composite the tiny person into/onto it.

## Recurring devices catalog

1. Miniature-self via overlay composite (the signature).
2. Giant-hand foreground forced perspective.
3. Object scaling (giant mundane objects + tiny person).
4. Tiny-person-in-mundane-location (sink, elevator, bed, bag).

## Production rules for TRIPPEDD segments

- Phone + tripod + CapCut. No crew, no paid tools, no green screen to buy.
- Deadpan performance; the trick is presented as real and mundane.
- Lowercase caption, a few words, emoji; always tag the network's standard tags.
- In-character comment replies extend the joke — the comments are part of the segment.
- Source footage stays original (our people, our tables). Never repost another
  creator's clip for this format.
- Provenance: keep Take 1 / Take 2 / project notes linkable per the
  Physical Source Timeline rule in TRIPPEDD-FORMAT.md.
