# AD RESEARCH — Network IDs, Bumpers, and the Adult Swim Playbook

*Owner directive 2026-10-10: research the most popular ads of the last 20 years and make our ads/commercials better. This doc is the TRIPPEDD NETWORK application — IDs, bumpers, cards, and promos.*

## The campaigns that matter (2006–2026)

### 1. Adult Swim bumps — the master class (2001–present)
The most imitated interstitial grammar in television: white text on black (or the reverse), deadpan one-liners, surreal non-sequiturs, no logo animation, no voiceover — just a card and a feeling. Sometimes funny ("You are watching Adult Swim"), sometimes bleak ("[No More Bumps]" in tiny print). The anti-ad: it refuses to sell, which is why it sells the network harder than any promo.
**Why it works:**
- **Calm inside chaos.** The plain card is the continuity anchor between loud shows — this is exactly the owner's house-font law (generic backbone, custom spice).
- **Brevity as confidence.** A 5-second card with one sentence trusts the viewer to get it.
- **Music discovery engine.** Bumps paired with J Dilla, Flying Lotus, MF DOOM, Freddie Gibbs — the network became a tastemaker. Our bumpers should do the same for our own music.
- **The 2021 TikTok revival.** Vano 3000's "AS" trend (BadBadNotGood's "Time Moves Slow") had millions making their own Adult Swim bumps. The format is a meme template — ours should be too.

### 2. Old Spice — "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like" (2010)
Single-take absurdity, direct address, humor-first. Sales doubled; the Response Campaign (180 personalized videos in 3 days) invented the "brand answers the internet" format.
**What we steal:** our network IDs can answer the audience. A "STAY TRIPPEDD" card that responds to comments, trends, or current events is the Response Campaign as a bumper format.

### 3. Geico — "It's What You Do" (1999–present)
25+ years of the same joke skeleton with new casting. Consistency builds a brand voice the audience can imitate.
**What we steal:** the TRIPPEDD card format should be FIXED (same timing, same deadpan energy, same end beat) while the text and visuals rotate. Familiarity is what makes a bumper quotable.

### 4. Snickers — "You're Not You When You're Hungry" (2010–)
One insight, infinite executions. The format never changes; the casting does.
**What we steal:** one network sentence ("STAY TRIPPEDD"), infinite contexts. The line stays; everything around it changes.

### 5. TikTok ad grammar (2020–2026)
**Hook (0–3s) → Body (4–15s) → CTA (last 3–5s).** Pattern interrupts. When creative fatigues, swap the hook, keep the body. Native/UGC style beats polished.
**What we steal:** our Shorts ARE the bumpers now. Every card should work as a 5–8 second vertical Short with zero modification.

### 6. Apple minimalism / "Think Different"
Confidence through restraint. The less you say, the more each word weighs.
**What we steal:** the black-background white-text cards the owner asked for ARE this. Don't decorate them — let the sentence carry it.

### 7. Nike storytelling
Mythic stakes, ordinary people. "Just Do It" is three words that never needed explaining.
**What we steal:** show promos should give characters mythic stakes in one line. Not plot — aura.

## The Adult Swim bump formula, applied to TRIPPEDD NETWORK
The grammar, beat by beat:
1. **One card. One sentence.** Never two ideas in one bumper.
2. **Deadpan or surreal, never earnest.** The network voice is the friend who says the weird thing flatly. ("STAY TRIPPEDD." Full stop.)
3. **Plain type for the anchor cards** (the continuity backbone — owner's law). Custom house fonts for the flavor cards.
4. **Music is half the bumper.** Every card gets a real track — ours or licensed-free — and the music should be discoverable (title cards for tracks, like AS did for Dilla/FlyLo).
5. **End on the network name or don't.** AS often ended on nothing but the feeling. Our rule: every third bumper ends on "TRIPPEDD NETWORK"; the rest end on the vibe.
6. **Make the format a meme template.** If a fan can't remake it in 30 seconds, it's too complicated. The 2021 AS TikTok trend proved the template IS the marketing.

## Bumper rotation system (mirrors the font commercial rotation)
- **Anchor cards** (plain, generic font, black bg): schedule announcements, "WE'LL BE RIGHT BACK," warnings.
- **Flavor cards** (custom house fonts, trippy backgrounds): "STAY TRIPPEDD," jokes, surreal bits.
- **Music cards:** track title + artist over a vibe visual — our version of the AS music-discovery bump.
- Rotate text; never rotate the format.

## Techniques to steal — checklist for every network ID / bumper / promo
- [ ] One card, one sentence. If there are two ideas, make two bumpers.
- [ ] Deadpan delivery — flat, confident, never begging for attention.
- [ ] Anchor cards use the plain continuity font; flavor cards use house fonts. Never the reverse.
- [ ] Works as a vertical Short with zero changes (5–8s, 9:16 safe).
- [ ] Music credited and discoverable — the bumper is a tastemaker, not wallpaper.
- [ ] Format is fixed; text/visuals rotate (Geico rule).
- [ ] Ends on the network name every third bumper; vibe the rest.
- [ ] Fan-remakeable in under a minute — the format is the meme.
- [ ] No fake anything: real shows, real dates, real music. A bumper that lies kills trust in all of them.
- [ ] CTA only when there's somewhere to go. A bumper with no destination ends on the vibe, not a dead link.
