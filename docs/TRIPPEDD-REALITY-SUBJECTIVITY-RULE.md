# TRIPPEDD Reality / Subjectivity Rule

## Canonical principle
TRIPPEDD has two simultaneous realities:

1. **Physical reality** — what the camera/source evidence establishes happened.
2. **Subjective reality** — what the audience is allowed to experience through the character's altered perception.

The audience may see the world become completely impossible while the character later treats the experience as insignificant, ordinary, or not even a real trip.

## The signature transition

The visual language may evolve:

`LIVE ACTION → DISTORTION → ANIMATION → 3D / BLENDER → IMPOSSIBLE WORLD → ??? → LIVE ACTION`

This transition is not merely a visual-effects showcase. It represents the character's subjective experience becoming the language of the episode.

## The return-to-reality joke

When the sequence ends, the character does **not** have to validate what the audience just saw.

The preferred comedy pattern is:

> Audience: **That was fucking insane.**
>
> Character: **“Nah, that shit wasn't even working.”**

This creates an implicit piece of world-building: the audience realizes that these characters may have an extremely high baseline for what they consider a real trip.

## EP01 application

For the pilot, the shroomified sequence can become the first major bridge from live-action source material into generated/animated/3D production language.

The sequence should be allowed to become visually enormous while remaining grounded in the character's eventual denial that anything meaningful happened.

The final lost-acid button then completes the contrast:

1. The shroomified experience appears to become surreal and technologically expansive.
2. The character returns to ordinary reality.
3. He dismisses it: **“That wasn't shit.”**
4. He remembers the acid he bought.
5. He cannot find it.
6. The entire day's attempt to trip ends in mundane sadness.
7. Cut to black.

The audience is left to infer: **if that wasn't a trip, what the hell counts as one to these people?**

## Production implications

### Source evidence
Physical-source records remain immutable and factual. Generated material must never be inserted into the physical timeline as if it actually occurred.

### Editorial assembly
The editor/director may interleave live-action source, generated material, animation, Blender/3D, VFX, sound design, and other formats as one subjective sequence while preserving provenance for every component.

### Generation eligibility
Generated material should be anchored to available source evidence where possible:
- character appearance
- location
- props
- camera position
- performance
- dialogue
- established visual motifs

### Continuity
The subjective sequence should return cleanly to the physical/editorial reality without requiring the character to explain or acknowledge the visual experience.

### Comedy priority
Do not let spectacle become the joke unless the scene specifically calls for it. The deeper joke is the mismatch between the audience's experience and the character's deadpan assessment.

## Studio rule
**The AI may make the world insane. The character gets to say it wasn't shit.**

This is a recurring TRIPPEDD storytelling capability and should be available to the episode director, generation planner, animation planner, and editorial QC systems.
