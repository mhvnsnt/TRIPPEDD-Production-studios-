## ASSET TYPE BREAKDOWN

### 1. Puppet Rigs (.fla) — Adobe Animate source files
**What they are:** Complete character rigs as editable Animate files. Every layer, every symbol, every timeline.

**File types:** `.fla` (source), sometimes with exported `.swf` previews

**What they teach:**
- Layer hierarchy design (how to nest 30+ body parts without chaos)
- Symbol nesting strategy (Graphic vs MovieClip for mouths/hands/eyes)
- Registration point placement (where joints pivot)
- Naming conventions that scale across a production
- Version control through filenames

**How agents use them:**
> When building a character rig for Wizard Gang/Goodville: open the Gumball
> puppet structure doc, follow the layer hierarchy template, name layers per
> the convention. Don't copy Gumball's shapes — copy the ORGANIZATION.

**B2 path:** `cn-gigaleak/[show]/puppet-rigs/`
**Priority:** HIGHEST

---

### 2. Show Bibles (PDF)
**What they are:** The canonical creative document. Character backgrounds, world rules, episode formulas, tone.

**File types:** `.pdf` (scanned, 20-50 pages typical)

**What they teach:**
- 5-section architecture (Overview → Characters → World → Premises)
- Character entry format (standardized questionnaires)
- How to write a series overview that sells the show in one page
- Story premise writing (title + 2-sentence springboard)

**How agents use them:**
> When writing a bible for Wizard Gang EP03 or a new show: follow the
> 5-section template. Use the Character Application form for every character.
> Write 8-12 premises to prove the concept works.

**B2 path:** `cn-gigaleak/[show]/bibles/`
**Priority:** HIGH
**Study note:** See `study-notes/bible-structure-analysis.md`

---

### 3. Model Sheets (PDF/image)
**What they are:** Character reference sheets — turnarounds, expressions, poses, color callouts.

**File types:** `.pdf`, `.png`, `.jpg` (high-res scans)

**What they teach:**
- How many views in a turnaround (front/34/side/back minimum)
- Expression sheet scope (how many emotions, which ones)
- Proportion notation (heads-tall, relative measurements)
- Color model format (exact hex/swatches, not "kinda blue")
- Construction guides (how characters are built from shapes)

**How agents use them:**
> When creating a model sheet for a new character: match the professional
> format. Turnaround → expressions → mouth chart → hands → color model.
> (You already have a solid template — compare against CN sheets and refine.)

**B2 path:** `cn-gigaleak/[show]/model-sheets/`
**Priority:** HIGH

---

### 4. Storyboards (PDF)
**What they are:** Panel-by-panel visual episode scripts.

**File types:** `.pdf` (multi-page, sometimes 200+ pages per episode)

**What they teach:**
- Panel layout conventions (how many per page, what size)
- Action notation (how movement is described in static panels)
- Dialogue placement (where text goes relative to art)
- Camera direction notation (pan, zoom, cut indicators)
- Board-driven vs script-driven differences

**How agents use them:**
> When boarding a Wizard Gang scene: follow the panel format. Number panels,
> notate camera moves, place dialogue below the panel it syncs to.

**B2 path:** `cn-gigaleak/[show]/storyboards/`
**Priority:** MEDIUM

---

### 5. Scripts (PDF/text)
**What they are:** Episode scripts in animation format.

**File types:** `.pdf`, `.txt`, `.fountain`

**What they teach:**
- Animation script format (denser action lines than live-action)
- How visual gags are written (they must work without dialogue)
- Page-to-minute ratios for 11-min vs 22-min formats

**How agents use them:**
> When writing a TRIPPEDD or Wizard Gang script: use animation format.
> Action lines carry the comedy, not just dialogue.

**B2 path:** `cn-gigaleak/[show]/scripts/`
**Priority:** MEDIUM

---

### 6. Brand Guides (PDF)
**What they are:** Visual identity documents — logos, colors, tone, usage rules.

**File types:** `.pdf`

**What they teach:**
- How to document a show's visual identity
- Color palette specification (exact values)
- Logo clear-space and misuse examples
- Tone-of-voice for marketing copy

**How agents use them:**
> When building a brand guide for TRIPPEDD network or a show: follow this
> format. Your shows need this for merch, thumbnails, and cross-promo.

**B2 path:** `cn-gigaleak/[show]/brand-guides/`
**Priority:** MEDIUM

---

### 7. Pitch Documents (PDF)
**What they are:** Documents used to sell shows to the network.

**File types:** `.pdf`

**What they teach:**
- Logline craft (one sentence that sells)
- Comp titles (positioning against known shows)
- Audience definition
- Visual pitch art presentation

**How agents use them:**
> When the owner wants to pitch a new show: this is the format.
> Study how CN shows positioned themselves.

**B2 path:** `cn-gigaleak/[show]/pitches/`
**Priority:** LOW (but useful for new show development)

---

### 8. Vehicle/Prop Model Sheets
**What they are:** Turnarounds for non-character assets (cars, props, locations).

**File types:** `.pdf`, `.png`

**What they teach:**
- Props get the same turnaround treatment as characters
- Consistent angle conventions

**How agents use them:**
> When modeling props for games or shows: build prop sheets the same way.

**B2 path:** `cn-gigaleak/[show]/model-sheets/props/`
**Priority:** LOW
